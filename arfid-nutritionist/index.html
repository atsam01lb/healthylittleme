ARFID, or Avoidant/Restrictive Food Intake Disorder, is an eating disorder marked by a limited range of accepted foods, driven by sensory sensitivity, fear of choking or vomiting, or simply a lack of interest in eating — rather than by concerns about weight or body shape. Left unsupported, it can lead to nutrient gaps, slowed growth, and significant stress around mealtimes for the whole family.

Because ARFID looks different in every child, treatment starts with a full picture of what, how much, and why your child eats the way they do, then builds forward from there at a pace they can actually tolerate.

Clinical Distinction

ARFID vs. Picky Eating

It's not just "being a picky eater."

Most young children go through a picky eating phase — it's a normal part of development and usually improves with time. ARFID is different in scope and severity: it involves a persistently narrow list of "safe" foods, real nutritional or growth consequences, and mealtime distress that doesn't resolve on its own. If picky eating is affecting your child's weight, energy, nutrient intake, or ability to eat outside the home, it's worth a closer clinical look rather than waiting for it to pass.

How It Works

How ARFID Nutrition Therapy Works

A structured, patient process built around your child's specific sensory and anxiety-related barriers to eating.

Individualized Assessment

A full review of your child's eating history, accepted foods, growth, and any related medical or sensory factors.

Gradual, Sensory-Informed Exposure

Small, manageable steps toward new foods and textures, at a pace that respects your child's comfort level.

Family Coaching & Mealtime Support

Practical strategies for parents and caregivers to reduce mealtime stress and reinforce progress at home.

Coordination with Therapists & Physicians

Nutrition care stays connected to any feeding therapists, occupational therapists, or physicians already involved.
